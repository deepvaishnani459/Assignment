Non-contact optical assessment of microcirculation in the diabetic foot.

Microcirculatory impairment is a central, often underassessed driver of diabetic foot complications. This briefing presents four non-contact optical technologies — LSCI, HSI, NIRS and IRT — with evidence graded against the 2023 IWGDF/ESVS/SVS intersocietal guidelines.1

Section 1 · Background

Why assess microcirculation?

Microcirculation — the network of arterioles, capillaries and venules below 150µm — governs oxygen and nutrient delivery to tissues. In diabetes, this network is fundamentally compromised.

Interactive capillary network simulation — hover to explore

Definition

Diabetes-Related Microangiopathy

Formally defined by the 2023 IWGDF/ESVS/SVS intersocietal guidelines as pathological structural and functional changes in the microcirculation occurring in any part of the body as a consequence of diabetes mellitus.113

Clinical Gap

Limitations of Standard Tests

ABI is unreliable with arterial calcification — common in diabetic patients. Toe pressures may be technically difficult. Both tests measure macrovascular flow and frequently miss significant small-vessel disease, particularly when medial arterial calcification is present.1

Risk

~50% of DFU Patients Have PAD

The presence of peripheral artery disease markedly increases the risk of amputation and cardiovascular mortality.1 Identifying microcirculatory impairment early is crucial for intervention timing and operative planning.

Opportunity

The Clinical Question

Not only whether a large vessel is occluded, but whether sufficient capillary-level perfusion exists to support tissue repair — this is what optical technologies uniquely address without contact, contrast, or ionising radiation.

Section 2 · Pathophysiology

From hyperglycaemia to non-healing ulcer

How chronic high glucose dismantles the microcirculation and why wounds stall at the capillary level.

01

Hyperglycaemia

Sustained high glucose damages endothelial cells and the glycocalyx lining capillaries, initiating a cascade of microvascular injury.

02

Microangiopathy

Basement membrane thickening, capillary rarefaction, and arteriolar hyalinosis reduce effective tissue perfusion.

03

Neuropathy & Inflammation

Loss of protective sensation combined with chronic inflammation delays the normal wound-healing cascade.

04

Tissue Hypoxia

Inadequate O₂ delivery stalls fibroblast proliferation, collagen synthesis, and immune function at the wound bed.

05

Non-Healing Ulcer

Ulceration, infection, and amputation risk rise sharply — compounded by co-existing PAD in ~50% of cases.1

Section 4 · Technology Review

Deep Dive: Four Optical Modalities

Select a modality to explore principle, evidence, clinical relevance, and limitations in detail.

Evidence: Emerging
LSCI
Laser Speckle Contrast Imaging

Principle: Coherent laser light illuminates tissue, generating an interference speckle pattern. Moving red blood cells blur this pattern proportionally to their velocity — quantified as speckle contrast — yielding real-time full-field maps of relative cutaneous perfusion without contact, contrast agents, or ionising radiation.

Measures: Relative cutaneous blood flow velocity (arbitrary perfusion units); dynamic responses to PORH and Buerger's test capture microvascular reactivity.

Does NOT measure: Absolute blood flow in standardised physiological units; tissue oxygenation; deep perfusion beyond ~1mm; cause of reduced perfusion.

✓ Advantages

  • Real-time full-field perfusion maps
  • No contact, contrast or radiation

✗ Limitations

  • Motion artefact is the major challenge
  • No absolute calibration units
  • Superficial only (~1mm depth)

Evidence Quality

Current evidence comprises small-to-moderate cohort studies and feasibility work. No large-scale diagnostic accuracy studies or RCTs have evaluated LSCI as a primary decision-making tool. No published guideline currently recommends LSCI as a standard assessment step.

📋 Key Clinical Evidence

Mennes et al. (2021) — Prospective cohort, n=61 DFU patients. LSCI distinguished non-ischaemic, ischaemic and critical-ischaemic patients. Healing association at 12 and 26 weeks. J Clin Med.2
Mennes et al. (2020) — Feasibility study, n=33 DFU. Validated semi-automatic tracking algorithm for PORH and Buerger's testing, identifying reproducible ROIs across foot zones. Diagnostics.3
Hsieh et al. (2024) — Object tracking-based motion-compensated LSCI for wound monitoring in patients unable to maintain still posture. APL Bioeng.4
Evidence: Moderate–Strong
HSI
Hyperspectral Imaging

Principle: Acquires images across tens to hundreds of narrow spectral bands spanning visible and near-infrared wavelengths. Exploits the known differential absorption spectra of oxyhaemoglobin and deoxyhaemoglobin to compute pixel-level tissue oxygen saturation (StO₂) maps across the entire wound surface in a single acquisition.

Measures: Spatial maps of OxyHb, DeoxyHb and derived StO₂. Some systems additionally report tissue water content and melanin index.

Does NOT measure: Blood flow velocity or volume; deep or muscular perfusion (depth limited to ~2–3mm); anatomical level of arterial obstruction.

~2–3 mm (papillary dermis)
Spatial Output

✗ Limitations

Evidence Quality

HSI has the strongest evidence base among the four modalities for DFU healing prediction, supported by a PROSPERO-registered systematic review and meta-analysis (2025).5 A 10-year systematic review (Sari et al., 2025) confirms no HSI-based method has yet achieved wide clinical adoption.10

📋 Key Clinical Evidence

González-Villacorta et al. (2025) — Systematic review & meta-analysis (PROSPERO CRD42023495391). 9 publications, 14 study arms. Promising DFU healing prognosis accuracy, I²=0. Microcirculation.5
Kounas et al. (2023) — Prospective, portable HSI post-debridement in DFU without PAD. Periwound OxyHb and O₂ saturation confirmed as potential healing biomarkers. Wound Repair Regen.6
López-Moral et al. (2022) — HSI vs. standard non-invasive vascular tests in DFU patients. HSI provides complementary — not superior — information to pressure-based modalities. J Vasc Surg.7
Sari et al. (2025) — 10-year systematic review: no HSI imaging method has yet achieved wide clinical adoption; highlights equity gap with darker skin tones. Front Med Technol.10
Evidence: Moderate (Limited by Study Size)
NIRS
Near-Infrared Spectroscopy

Principle: Exploits the relative transparency of biological tissue to near-infrared light (700–900nm). Multiple wavelengths detect differential absorption between oxygenated and deoxygenated haemoglobin. Some systems use continuous-wave mode; others use spatial or frequency-domain approaches for improved depth sensitivity.

Measures: Regional tissue StO₂, total haemoglobin (HbT), oxyhaemoglobin area under the curve (O₂HbAUC), oxygen consumption (VO₂), and dynamic deoxygenation/reoxygenation during limb occlusion or exercise — as comprehensively documented by Joseph et al.8

Does NOT measure: Spatially resolved maps (most systems are point-measurement); anatomical level of arterial occlusion; blood flow velocity directly.

✓ Advantages

  • Systematic review support (PAD/DFD)
  • Physiologically grounded depth metrics
  • Deeper penetration than HSI and LSCI
  • Emerging smartphone-based systems

✗ Limitations

  • Regional average only, no spatial map
  • Signal contaminated by fat, muscle, bone
  • No validated DFU-specific thresholds
  • Limited validation in diverse populations
Evidence Quality

NIRS has a plausible physiological basis and systematic review-level support for PAD assessment.8 Clinical validation studies specifically in DFU healing prediction are few, largely retrospective, and underpowered. No diagnostic accuracy threshold has been validated prospectively.

📋 Key Clinical Evidence

Joseph et al. (2022) — Systematic review: NIRS in PAD and diabetic foot. Documents StO₂, HbT, VO₂, O₂HbAUC metrics. Utility in stratifying high-risk limbs where neuropathy masks ischaemic symptoms. Diabetes Metab Res Rev.8
Moritz et al. (2023) — Point-of-care SnapshotNIR tissue oxygenation assessment; patterns associated with wound closure vs. non-healing. Plast Reconstr Surg Glob Open.9
Sari et al. (2025) — 10-year systematic review confirming emerging smartphone-based NIRS systems for DFU monitoring with combined thermal mapping. Front Med Technol.10
Evidence: Moderate (Monitoring Use)
IRT
Infrared Thermography

Principle: All objects above absolute zero emit infrared radiation proportional to their temperature. IRT cameras detect emitted IR and produce quantitative, non-contact skin surface temperature maps. The primary clinical metric is temperature asymmetry — typically >2.2°C — between corresponding bilateral foot regions as a flag for underlying pathology.

Measures: Skin surface temperature (°C). Bilateral temperature asymmetry is the primary clinical index.

Critical distinction — Temperature ≠ Perfusion: IRT measures surface temperature as an indirect reflection of underlying blood flow, metabolic activity, and ambient conditions. Elevated temperature may indicate inflammation or infection rather than improved perfusion; reduced temperature may reflect ischaemia, vasoconstriction, or cold ambient exposure.

Evidence Quality

IRT has the longest clinical history and the largest body of observational evidence (Piva et al. 2022, n=1,078).12 Evidence is moderate for monitoring/screening in high-risk diabetic feet. The 2024 Sciachitano systematic review confirmed value for DFU risk prediction but highlighted absence of a universally validated temperature threshold.13

📋 Key Clinical Evidence

Piva et al. (2022) — Systematic review, 21 studies, n=1,078. IRT useful for monitoring effects of revascularisation, pharmacological therapy and exercise rehabilitation in PAD. Diagnostics.12
Faus Camarena et al. (2024) — Bibliographic review: IRT confirmed as effective complementary tool for pre-ulcerous detection and DFU monitoring; consistent evidence for earlier clinical intervention. Sensors.11
Schaper et al. (IWGDF 2023) — Practical guidelines update. No optical modality is included as a recommended primary assessment test for PAD or wound perfusion in the diabetic foot.13

⚠️ Shared Critical Limitations Across All Four Modalities

Universal

No Validated Thresholds

Absence of agreed diagnostic cut-off values prevents standardised treatment decisions and inter-operator comparison for all four modalities.

Universal

Limited Prospective Data

Most evidence derives from small, single-centre observational studies. Prospective, multicentre, adequately powered trials with clinical endpoints are lacking for all four modalities.

Equity

Ethnic Diversity Gap

Melanin absorption overlaps haemoglobin spectra in HSI and NIRS. Most validation studies were conducted in predominantly light-skinned populations — a critical equity limitation given diabetes prevalence in minority groups.

Current Positioning

Adjuncts, Not Replacements

No optical modality is yet endorsed by the 2023 IWGDF/ESVS/SVS intersocietal guidelines.113 All should be interpreted as complementary to — not replacing — established vascular assessment pathways including ABI, toe pressure, TcPO₂, and clinical judgement.

Critical Gap

Standardisation Required

Validated diagnostic thresholds, standardised acquisition protocols, multicentre prospective trials with clinical endpoints, and inclusion of ethnically diverse populations are the essential next steps before any modality can be recommended for routine clinical practice.

Section 11 · Limitations

Limitations Laboratory

Shared constraints across all four modalities. Understanding these barriers to clinical translation is mandatory before interpreting any optical measurement.1

Motion Artefact

Significant challenge for LSCI (worst affected) and HSI. Patients with painful wounds may not maintain required stillness. Semi-automatic tracking algorithms partially mitigate this for LSCI,3 with further improvement shown by Hsieh et al. (2024).4

Skin Pigmentation

Affects HSI signal calibration (melanin absorption overlaps haemoglobin spectra) and NIRS readings. Most validation studies were conducted in predominantly light-skinned populations — a major equity limitation given the higher diabetes prevalence in some ethnic minority groups.10

Ambient Environment

Critical confounder for IRT — temperature, draughts, humidity and recent activity all confound measurements.12 Standardised acclimatisation periods (typically 10–15 minutes) are required but rarely enforced consistently across sites.

No Validated Cut-offs

Without agreed thresholds, findings cannot trigger standardised treatment decisions and inter-operator comparison is unreliable.1 This is a fundamental barrier to clinical translation for all four modalities and the single most important unmet need.

Device Variability

Different manufacturers produce devices with different light sources, sensors, and processing algorithms. Results from one device cannot be reliably compared to another, even within the same modality — preventing cross-study comparison and limiting meta-analytic confidence.5

Depth Limitation

All four modalities are superficially weighted: LSCI penetrates ~1mm; HSI ~2–3mm; NIRS is deeper but imprecise depending on tissue composition.8 Deep tissue or macrovascular ischaemia may not be detected — requiring complementary standard imaging.

Section 13 · Clinician Summary

Explained to a clinician in five lines

A distillation of the key take-home messages for busy clinicians encountering these technologies for the first time.

01

These four technologies allow us to look at blood flow, oxygen levels and temperature in the wound and surrounding foot without touching the skin — important because standard ankle pressure tests frequently miss small-vessel disease in diabetic patients, particularly where arterial calcification renders ABI unreliable.1

02

Hyperspectral imaging maps oxygen levels across the whole wound in a single image and currently has the strongest evidence for predicting whether a diabetic foot ulcer will heal — a 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis (14 study arms, I²=0) supports its use as a healing prognostic adjunct when planning revascularisation or conservative-versus-operative decisions.567

03

Laser speckle and near-infrared spectroscopy can show real-time perfusion and regional oxygen saturation respectively, but the evidence in diabetic foot ulcers remains early-stage — they are useful research and adjunctive tools today, not yet validated clinical decision-making instruments that should independently trigger treatment changes.2389

04

Thermography is the most established of the four and is good at flagging early warning signs of inflammation and monitoring the response to treatment — but a warm foot does not always mean good perfusion. Infection, Charcot neuroarthropathy and postoperative hyperaemia can produce identical thermographic appearances, so always interpret temperature findings in full clinical context.1112 13

05

All four technologies share the same fundamental limitation: we do not yet have agreed cut-off values or enough large prospective trials to use them as standalone decision-making tools. Treat them as promising adjuncts that add mechanistically credible information to — rather than replace — the standard vascular assessment pathway endorsed by the 2023 IWGDF/ESVS/SVS guidelines.113
